# Problem statement

## The people affected

Port shift supervisors coordinate vessel arrivals, berths, cranes, yard capacity, and priority cargo under continuous change. A delayed vessel or reduced berth capacity can cascade into missed connections, excess anchorage time, and expensive demurrage.

## Why the current approach fails

The planning inputs commonly live in disconnected schedule exports, emails, and spreadsheets. These tools can list arrivals but do not make the trade-offs explicit: which vessel should move first, how much risk a delay creates, or which action protects a cold-chain shipment. By the time a queue is visible, the useful intervention window may be gone.

## Why this matters now

The provided challenge notes that the 2021 LA/Long Beach backlog exceeded 100 waiting ships and imposed an estimated $10B supply-chain cost. The same underlying planning challenge remains: operators need early, defensible recommendations that combine capacity and urgency rather than a retrospective chart.

## Scope

HarborLens focuses on a supervisor's next 72 hours. It does not autonomously control a terminal; it makes a recommendation, shows its evidence, and leaves the decision with the operator.
